What the model is trained on

Ten thousand simulated episodes of a sphere of sand thrown into a walled box. Each episode records 1,024 material points for 1.2 seconds at 2.5 ms per frame — the point cloud below is the training signal, frame by frame. Twelve episodes are shown, chosen to span the range of launches the model sees.

Coverage of the training set

Every episode draws a scenario — drop, wall or slam — then a random position, orientation, launch velocity and spin. Each panel below is one scenario, plotted against the two things that scenario actually varies: its launch strength and its initial spin. The pale cloud is all 10,000 training episodes; the dark circles are the twelve shown here.

The vertical axis differs by scenario because the scenarios sample different things: a drop is released at rest, so its energy is the height it falls from, while a wall throw and a slam are defined by their launch speed. Spin is uniform on 0–12 rad/s about a random axis in all three. The twelve sit at the corners of each panel by design — the extremes of gentle/energetic and slow/fast spin — so the set spans the box rather than sampling its middle twelve times.

Twelve episodes, same clock

All twelve play in lockstep on physical time and share one camera — drag anywhere in the grid to orbit them together. Click a panel to enlarge it.

Sand is drawn at 1,024 points, the same subsample the model is trained on; the box is 1 m across, the floor grid 10 cm. Every fourth simulator frame is shown (10 ms apart), so playback is smooth without loading every step.

Height, speed and spin are the launch conditions. “Hits floor” and “settles” are the frames where the first point reaches the floor and where motion drops below 5 cm/s; “final spread” is the width of the sand at 1.2 s (90th-percentile diameter about its centre). Together they show how differently these episodes end.
